## Three robust findings

1. **Retrieval is decisive at every scale.** C1→C2 lifts ES by 0.27–0.46 and EM several-fold on both datasets, for all three models. The no-retrieve baselines are "correctly low" (knowledge-limited), not broken.

2. **Model size: accuracy ↑, hallucination ↓ monotonically.** C1 ES 0.5B<1.5B<7B; C1 invented-hallucination 0.5B>1.5B>7B (RepoEval-fn hall_A4B2: 0.117 / 0.070 / 0.024). Smaller models know less and invent more.

3. **The cascade (C4) ≈ matches CARD (C3) on accuracy while cutting invented-identifier hallucination — at every scale.** At low T_RAG (where CARD is conservative) the static gate adds a small retrieval budget and drives hall_A4B2 toward ~0:

   The asymmetry guarantee holds throughout (C4 retr% ≥ C3, C4 ES ≥ C3, C4 hall ≤ C3).

## Secondary observations
- **CARD gate smoothness scales with model size/calibration.** 7B and 0.5B (self-calibrated) give graded retrieval curves; 1.5B's gate is sharper (0→100 over T_RAG 0.3–0.5). Better-calibrated logits → more tunable adaptive retrieval.
- **Latency** scales with model size (CCE C1: 199 / 200 / 502 ms for 0.5B/1.5B/7B). The cascade's hallucination reduction comes at single-digit-% extra retrieval (near-C1 latency), far below C2's always-retrieve cost — the cost/quality sweet spot.
- **body vs full (RepoEval-fn):** dedent body-truncation is essential — full scoring is deflated by over-generation past the body (e.g. 7B C2 ES 0.839 body vs 0.444 full).

## Bottom line
The static-analysis cascade delivers its intended contribution — **near-elimination of invented-identifier hallucinations that CARD's confidence gate misses, at minimal extra retrieval cost, without hurting accuracy — across 0.5B, 1.5B, and 7B.** The effect is largest (absolute) where hallucination is largest (smaller models), and cleanest to tune where the gate is best-calibrated (7B).
